## Line 1 — Hook (Frame 1)

**Time:** 0.0 – 8.0s
**Delivery:** Flat statement, then the turn lands the point.

    Everyone at this table is lying to you. In Coup: Rebellion G54, that's not a problem — that's the entire point.

## Line 2 — First game (Frame 2)

**Time:** 8.0 – 21.0s
**Delivery:** Promise first, then the facts like dealing cards.

    Your first game, start to finish. G54 is a standalone bluffing game — three to six players, fifteen minutes. You each get two face-down cards. Every card is a life, and a power you can claim. Truth is optional.

## Line 3 — The twist (Frame 3)

**Time:** 21.0 – 33.0s
**Delivery:** "Here's the twist" earns a small lift; count the categories cleanly.

    Here's the twist. The box holds twenty-five roles — but every game uses only five: one finance, one communications, one force, two special interest. Those five sit face up, public, all game long.

## Line 4 — Setup (Frame 4)

**Time:** 33.0 – 44.0s
**Delivery:** Quick and practical; read the five role names distinctly.

    Setup takes a minute. Two coins each; the rest is the Treasury. Two cards each; the rest is the Court deck. For game one, run the starter set — Banker, Director, Guerrilla, Politician, Peacekeeper. Last winner starts; turns run clockwise.

## Line 5 — The turn (Frame 5)

**Time:** 44.0 – 56.0s
**Delivery:** Signposting rhythm: one action… or claim… then the clock.

    Your turn: one action, no passing. Income — take one coin. Or claim a role — no proof needed. Reach ten coins, and Coup is your only move: seven coins, unblockable, one life gone.

## Line 6 — Challenges (Frame 6)

**Time:** 56.0 – 67.0s
**Delivery:** Symmetric risk, even tone; "still hidden" is the payoff word.

    Anyone can challenge any claim — even someone not involved. Catch a bluff: the claimant loses a life. Wrong call: you lose one instead. Truth proven: the card flips back into the Court, and a fresh one replaces it. Still hidden.

## Line 7 — Blocks (Frame 7)

**Time:** 67.0 – 78.0s
**Delivery:** Counterintuitive rule, no rush; the self-block line is the hook of the frame.

    Under attack? Only the target can block. Blocks are claims too — they can be lies. And in G54, force roles block themselves: Guerrilla blocks Guerrilla. A blocked attack still costs the attacker their coins.

## Line 8 — Losing influence (Frame 8)

**Time:** 78.0 – 89.0s
**Delivery:** Strategic weight; "reveal as little as you can" is advice, not trivia.

    Lose a life, and you choose which card flips face-up — reveal as little as you can. A bad challenge plus a real attack can cost two lives in one turn. Out of cards? Hand your coins to the Treasury.

## Line 9 — Landing (Frame 9)

**Time:** 89.0 – 101.0s
**Delivery:** Slow down for the win condition; the URL is a plain invitation, not an ad read.

    Five roles. Fifteen minutes. Fifty-six twenty-five combinations — a different game every night. Last player with a face-down card wins. There is no second place. Now run your first game — online at boardgame dot falent dot workers dot dev.
