SOURCE OF INFORMATION — distilled from this repo's rules research
(docs/research/coup-rebellion-g54/*.md), the material the game implementation is built on.

WHAT IT IS
- Coup: Rebellion G54 ("G54"), 2014, designer Rikki Tahta, published by La Mame Games
  and Indie Boards and Cards. Standalone game, NOT an expansion — owning base Coup is
  not required.
- Bluffing and deduction card game for 3-6 players (best 4-6; 2 players not supported;
  Kickstarter edition with 4 copies per role supports 7-8). About 15 minutes per game.
  Age 14+. "Easy to learn." Political-dystopia art.
- Engine reuses classic Coup: hidden influence cards are lives; claims may be lies;
  anything can be challenged; lost challenge costs a life; last player with a face-down
  card wins. No scoring, no second place, game ends the instant one player remains.

THE G54 TWIST — VARIABLE ROLE SETUP
- Box holds 25 role cards. Every game uses only 5: 1 Finance + 1 Communications +
  1 Force + 2 Special Interest. That makes 5x5x5x45 = 5,625 legal combinations, and
  each combination plays differently.
- Finance = money. Communications = card swap/filter. Force = remove influence for
  coins. Special Interest = negotiation, protection, reactive effects.
- The influence deck is 15 cards: 3 copies of each of the 5 active roles.
- The 5 active role cards sit face up, public, all game — they and the 2 general
  action cards are the only legal actions.
- Suggested first-game "starter set": Banker (Finance), Director (Communications),
  Guerrilla (Force), Politician + Peacekeeper (Special Interest). Advanced roles
  (Spy, Speculator, Writer, Producer, General, Mercenary, Communist, Customs Officer,
  Missionary, Intellectual, Lawyer, Protestor...) are saved for later games.

SETUP (from the official steps)
1. Put the 2 general action cards (Income, Coup) in the center.
2. Choose 5 role cards by category (random or draft) — beginner set excludes advanced roles.
3. Lay the 5 role cards face up so all players read them; pass them around.
4. Build the influence deck from those 5 roles (15 cards), shuffle, deal 2 face down to each player.
5. Remainder is the Court deck, face down, center.
6. Give each player 2 coins (public), rest is the Treasury. Last winner starts; turns go clockwise.

THE TURN
- One action per turn. No passing. Any action you can afford.
- 10 or more coins at the start of your turn: your ONLY legal action is Coup.
- General actions (always available, no claim, cannot be challenged or blocked):
  Income = take 1 coin. Coup = pay 7 coins, pick a target, target loses 1 influence
  immediately; always works, no challenge, no block.
- Role actions: claim a role (truth optional — proof only matters if challenged).
  If several linked claims resolve from one action, resolve clockwise from the active player.

CLAIMS, CHALLENGES, COUNTERS
- Challenge: any player, even uninvolved, may challenge any role claim or counter.
  One challenge resolves fully before play continues. General actions can never be challenged.
- Resolution: the challenged player shows a real matching card (then shuffles it into the
  Court and draws a replacement — identity stays hidden) or declines. Show = challenger
  loses 1 life. Decline/cannot = claimant loses 1 life and the whole action fails; costs
  paid for the failed action are returned.
- Multiple challenges: first come, first served; ties break clockwise from active player.
- Counteraction (block): the target claims a role to defend. May be a lie; may be challenged.
  Unchallenged counter succeeds. Default rule: ONLY THE TARGET may block in self-defense
  (exceptions: Protestor funding, Capitalist collection, General multi-block, Priest).
- A successful block stops the loss or theft but does NOT refund the attacker's coins
  (e.g. Judge pays 3, block succeeds, the 3 stays paid).
- In G54 each Force role is blocked by ITSELF (Guerrilla blocks Guerrilla, Judge blocks
  Judge) — unlike base Coup there are no guaranteed cross-role answers, because the
  active role set varies every game.

LOSING INFLUENCE AND ELIMINATION
- Face-down cards are lives. On each life loss, the LOSER chooses which of their cards
  to turn face up. Revealed cards stay face up and grant no power. Hidden info never leaks.
- Zero face-down cards = eliminated immediately; return all coins to the Treasury.
- Double life loss in one turn is legal: challenge an attack and lose (+1), suffer the
  execution anyway (+1). Same with a false block that gets caught.
- Reactive timing exists (Missionary, Intellectual after a loss; Lawyer on elimination)
  — advanced territory, first games use the starter set.

STARTER SET ROLES (teach these five)
- Banker (Finance): take 3 coins. No target, no block.
- Director (Communications): draw 2 from Court, return any 2. No block.
- Guerrilla (Force): pay 4, choose a target, target loses 1 life unless blocked;
  blocked only by Guerrilla (target only).
- Politician (Special Interest): steal up to 2 coins from a target; blocked by
  Politician (target only).
- Peacekeeper (Special Interest): take 1 coin and the Peacekeeping token — holder
  cannot be targeted by anything except Coup (challenges still allowed). One token;
  a new claim steals it.

TOKENS (in play only when their role is active)
Treaty (Foreign Consular allies cannot target each other, even by Coup, until final two),
Peacekeeping (Peacekeeper immunity except Coup), Disappear (Mercenary delayed kill after
target's next turn), Tax (Customs Officer: every claim of the marked role pays the holder 1).

TRUST RULES
Deals are allowed, promises are never binding, no showing hidden cards, no lending or
gifting coins unless a role forces it, use counters if coins run out, partial actions
allowed when coins or cards are short.
