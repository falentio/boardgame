# 01 Overview

## Identity

Name is Scape Goat. Designer is Jon Perry. Publisher is Indie Boards and Cards, and Lone Oak Games is the parent company behind that imprint. Release is 2020 [RB, JP, TT, DY, RD]. The rulebook carries the Lone Oak Games copyright for 2020 and gives the Indie Boards and Cards address for missing components [RB]. The credit line inside the rulebook lists graphic designer Bill Bricker, project manager Sydney Engelstein, developers Nick Little and Brian McCarthy, and artist Burr Johnson [RB].

Scape Goat is a social-deduction card game. It has two roles and no other role cards [SB]. A search for the title returns this product alone, so the name needs no disambiguation [RB, JP]. One unrelated title surfaced and was excluded. Alcatraz the Scapegoat is a semi-cooperative game with a different designer, publisher, and era [RB].

## Player count and session

The game supports 3 to 6 players [JP, DY, GNG, TT, RD]. The BoardGameGeek community rates it best with 4 to 5 [BGG-via-search]. Dale Yu found it acceptable at 3 players and expected it to improve at higher counts [DY].

Playing time is 30 minutes by the box figure [BGG-via-search, TT, DY]. Several reviews report 15 to 20 minutes in play [SB, RD]. No source gives a per-player-count breakdown [RB]. Both readings appear in `07-open-questions.md`.

The age rating is 10 plus per Tabletopia and 14 plus per Dale Yu [TT, DY]. The rulebook prints no age [RB]. The question is unresolved, and both readings appear in `07-open-questions.md`.

BoardGameGeek records a weight of 6.9 [TT].

## The two roles

One player is the scapegoat. Write the role with a lowercase s. Exactly one player is the scapegoat in a game, and that player does not know it [RB, RP, SB]. Every other player's own player mat names the scapegoat as the suspect. The scapegoat's own player mat names a different player instead [SB]. No player ever reads their own colour as their suspect [SB].

Every other player is a member of the table. The game has no other roles, no role cards, and no hidden role assignments [SB, RB].

## Win condition and scoring

The game has no points, no score track, and no player elimination [RB, RP, GNG]. Two parties can win, the scapegoat and everyone else.

The scapegoat wins when any player goes to the cops [RB, RP]. The scapegoat also wins when a frame attempt successfully frames a player who is not the scapegoat [RB, RP].

Everyone else wins only when a frame attempt successfully frames the true scapegoat [RB, RP]. The scapegoat loses only in that case.

The one true fact the game depends on is that exactly one scapegoat exists in a game, and every player mat except the scapegoat's own agrees on who it is [SB].

## The core loop

A turn has three parts in order. You move your player token to a new location, you take that location's action, and you swap one evidence card [RB, RP]. `03-turn-structure.md` covers the parts in detail.

Players talk freely between turns. They may state what cards they hold and may lie about it [RB, RP]. The design tension is that giving too much away to the scapegoat lets the scapegoat learn the target and run to the cops [RB, RP, SB, DY].

The table builds toward a frame attempt. A player who holds a preparation token can take the Frame action on the flipped Prepare card. A successful frame ends the game and names the framed player [RB, RP]. `04-frame-attempt-and-end.md` covers the procedure.

## The two end conditions

The game ends in exactly one of two ways [RB].

A frame attempt succeeds and frames a player. The game ends at once, and the scoring decides the winner.

A player goes to the cops. The game ends at once, and the scapegoat wins whoever ran.

There is no third end condition. The rulebook gives no turn limit, no deck-exhaustion end, and no stalemate rule [RB]. What happens if the deck or a supply runs dry is unresolved and appears in `07-open-questions.md`.
